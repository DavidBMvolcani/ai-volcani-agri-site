AutoTraps: Fruit Flies Detection

Smart traps that count fruit flies by species, in Israel and in Florida and Puerto Rico.

Project objective

Real-time pest diagnostics for growers

To deploy a network of fully automated digital traps (AutoTraps) equipped with computer vision and machine learning algorithms to detect, identify, and count destructive fruit flies, specifically targeting high-risk species such as Anastrepha, and to deliver real-time population diagnostics directly to growers.

Background & motivation

One of the greatest threats to global fruit production

Fruit flies of the Tephritidae family, including the aggressive Anastrepha, Bactrocera and Ceratitis genera, represent one of the single greatest biosecurity threats to global fruit production. Attacking high-value export crops like mangoes, melons, citrus and stone fruits, these pests cause billions of dollars in direct agricultural losses and enforce strict international trade quarantine barriers on growers.

Monitoring these insect populations traditionally relies on a highly manual, flawed workflow:

  • The labor bottleneck: Farmers place hundreds of physical plastic traps across large acreage. Field technicians must manually walk the orchards week after week to inspect traps, count dead insects and log paper data. This process is incredibly slow and expensive.
  • The delayed response gap: Because human inspection only happens every few days or weeks, a minor pest infestation can balloon into an uncontrollable population explosion before anyone notices.
  • The species identification problem: Telling different fruit fly species apart, or distinguishing them from harmless insects, requires trained entomological expertise. Mistaken identities lead to delayed or incorrect chemical treatments.
Our solution

Real-time edge analytics with AutoTraps

Our center bridges the gap between field agronomy and artificial intelligence by digitizing orchard biosecurity. The AutoTrap system completely automates pest management using a multi-layered computer vision pipeline:

  1. Autonomous capture & imagingAttracted by targeted food baits or synthetic pheromones, incoming fruit flies enter the low-power smart trap enclosure, where a miniaturized, high-resolution internal camera automatically takes their picture.
  2. Neural network identificationEach trap is equipped with an integrated Raspberry Pi 4 that securely stores the captured images and transmits them to a central server. There, an advanced machine learning classification model analyzes precise morphological markers, such as the unique, intricate wing patterns of the fruit fly, to identify the exact pest species.
  3. Instant GIS mappingThe trap automatically tallies the population and sends a daily update with exact GPS coordinates and infestation counts straight to a web dashboard or a farmer's mobile application.

By transforming biosecurity into a continuous digital feed, our system allows agricultural teams to spot the very first fly of a major outbreak. Growers can apply localized, targeted pesticide treatments only where and when they are truly needed, drastically lowering chemical usage, protecting the environment and saving significant operating costs.

Field deployments

Two deployments, two trap types

Florida and Puerto Rico

  • Target: Anastrepha fruit flies.
  • Trap: the Cervantes Agritech smart trap.
  • With: Prof. Daniel Carrillo (University of Florida) and Cervantes Agritech.

Israel

  • Targets: the peach fruit fly (Bactrocera zonata) and the Mediterranean fruit fly (Ceratitis capitata).
  • Trap: a camera trap imaging a yellow sticky card.
  • With: D.Sc. Victor Alchanatis and Dr. David Nestel (Volcani).
Results

Model detections on trap images

The detection model

A Faster R-CNN detection model

Our computer vision model is a deep neural network based on the Faster R-CNN architecture, which finds and names each fly in the image. It was fine-tuned on trap images annotated by entomologists.

The model keeps its accuracy under difficult field conditions, such as traps saturated with flies or images cluttered with non-target look-alike insects.
