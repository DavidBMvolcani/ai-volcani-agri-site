Anthocyanin Dynamics in Red Lettuces

Smart lighting for red lettuce: estimating the red pigment from hyperspectral and RGB images to guide LED lighting.

Project objective

Light that adapts to the plant

To develop an automated Decision Support System (DSS) that dynamically optimizes the greenhouse light environment, fine-tuning both spectral quality and light intensity, to precisely control and maximize the cultivation quality of red lettuce.

Background & motivation

Why red lettuce is hard to grow well

The study focuses on enhancing anthocyanin content in red lettuce, known for its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, as well as its potential anti-cancer effects. However, cultivating red lettuce at scale presents serious structural and natural challenges:

  • The spatial limitation of sunlight: Traditional farming relies heavily on natural sunlight. In modern, high-density greenhouse systems space is at a premium, and because of dense vertical stacking and structural shading it is practically impossible for every plant to receive direct, uniform sunlight.
  • The pigmentation challenge: The signature red color of the lettuce comes from a natural pigment called anthocyanin. To trigger its production the plant needs very specific light stress signals. Relying on unpredictable, fluctuating sunlight makes it impossible to guarantee a consistent, deeply colored crop.
Our solution

Closed-loop AI and synthetic lighting

To overcome the lack of natural space and sunlight, our center uses precise synthetic lighting networks of white and blue LED arrays. But simply turning the lights on is not enough. This project introduces a smart, data-driven system that bridges computer vision with greenhouse automation:

  1. ImagingHyperspectral and RGB cameras capture the lettuce plants.
  2. Anthocyanin estimationMachine learning models estimate how much anthocyanin, the red pigment, each plant shows in the images.
  3. Light decisionsThe decision support system uses these estimates to tune the white and blue LED light in the greenhouse.

By tuning the light spectrum to the plants' measured state, the system guides them toward the desired level of anthocyanin, so that every lettuce grows uniformly and becomes "red enough" to meet premium market standards.

The experiment

Four light treatments in a commercial greenhouse

Salanova lettuce, in red and green varieties, was grown in a commercial two-level hydroponic greenhouse in Bnei Atarot, central Israel. Several growth cycles were monitored under commercial conditions.

  • Imaging: hyperspectral and RGB.
  • Ground truth: weekly laboratory anthocyanin tests.
  • Experiment managers: D.Sc. Victor Alchanatis and Dr. Elena Vitoshkin, Institute of Agricultural and Biosystems Engineering.
  • Researchers: Prof. Nir Ohad, Tel Aviv University, and Dr. Ashutosh Mishra, Thapar Institute of Engineering and Technology, India.

Workflow

From images to an anthocyanin estimate

  1. Images to featuresHyperspectral and RGB images of each tray are split into single plants, and vegetation indices (NDVI, ARI, PSRI and more) are calculated per plant.
  2. Join with the labEach plant's image features are matched to its laboratory anthocyanin value.
  3. Train and compareRegression models (XGBoost, Random Forest and linear models) are tested within each trial and across trials.
