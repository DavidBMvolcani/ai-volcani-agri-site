MaltPrediction

Predicting malt-quality traits of barley lines from SWIR hyperspectral images of their kernels.

Project objective

Reading barley quality from the kernel's spectrum

The goal is to predict the rootlet protein of barley doubled-haploid (DH) lines, a malt-quality trait measured in the laboratory, from short-wave infrared (SWIR) hyperspectral images of their kernels.

The first stage, now complete, turns the raw images into calibrated reflectance spectra for every kernel, the data the prediction models will learn from.

  • Researchers: Dr. Ayal Friedman and D.Sc. Victor Alchanatis.
  • The AI center's role: built the calibration pipeline that turns the raw images into per-kernel spectra.
The data

Six weeks of SWIR imaging

Kernels were scanned between September and October 2026 on a push-broom SWIR platform: an overhead camera on a gantry, halogen lamps and a moving stage carrying the sample tray.

176barley DH lines imaged
526capture sessions
256SWIR bands, 938–2542 nm
148imaged lines with lab protein values

Grid trays

About 70 single kernels per tray, each in its own position, with two replicate trays per line: 351 sessions covering 175 lines. Each kernel gets its own spectrum.

Pile trays

A heap of several hundred touching kernels, scanned once per line: 175 sessions. The whole pile is one sample. Pile spectra differ systematically from grid spectra, so the two are kept as separate datasets.

Calibration pipeline

From raw image to reflectance, with nothing set by eye

Every reference is found automatically in each image, because the panels and tray move between campaigns. Each image contains its own references: dark lines at the start, and reflectance panels of 60% and 40% above and below the tray.

  1. DarkThe dark lines at the start of each scan give the sensor's dark level, per band and per column, which removes a fixed pattern across the sensor.
  2. Reference panelsThe two round reference panels are detected by size and shape; the widest line of the top (60%) panel becomes the white reference, column by column.
  3. Valid bandsOnly bands with a signal-to-noise ratio of at least 10 on the reference panel are kept.
  4. KernelsThe tray is located and every kernel is segmented; kernels fully under the reference panel are calibrated.
  5. ReflectanceEach kernel pixel is converted to reflectance, with stuck and saturated pixels excluded.
  6. OutputsA mean spectrum and a full reflectance cube for every kernel, plus a quality-control figure for every session.

Results so far

All grid sessions calibrated

  • 351 of 351 grid sessions calibrated (November 2026).
  • 14,662 kernel spectra, each with its full reflectance cube.
  • Calibration written as vectorised array maths, about 1000× faster than the original per-pixel loop, with identical results.
Next steps

From spectra to protein

The next stage links each line's kernel spectra to its laboratory rootlet-protein value and trains prediction models, with training and test sets split by line so that no line appears in both.
